Sources & methodology

Balance over time: the loan model

The chart and schedule use a fixed-rate, fully amortizing mortgage with monthly compounding. This is the standard annuity model used for U.S. fixed-rate home loans.

  • Monthly payment (principal & interest): M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−n), where P is the loan amount, r is the annual rate ÷ 12, and n is the number of monthly payments. At 0% interest it's simply P ÷ n.
  • Each month: interest = remaining balance × r; principal = M − interest; any extra payment goes entirely to principal. The final payment is reduced so the balance ends at exactly $0.
  • Lines on the chart (plotted at the end of each year): remaining balance; principal paid = loan amount − balance; interest paid = running total of interest; home value = price × (1 + g)years at your growth rate g; equity = home value − balance.
  • Simplifications: home value grows at a constant rate you choose (an assumption, not a forecast), property tax and insurance held flat, no refinancing, and amounts aren't rounded to the cent each month like a lender's statement (lifetime totals may differ by a few dollars).

PMI rules

PMI applies when the loan is more than 80% of the home price, priced as an annual percentage of the original loan amount. It ends at the earliest of: the original schedule reaching 78% of the home price (automatic), the actual balance reaching 80% (only if you request it), or the midpoint of the loan term. Source: Homeowners Protection Act, 12 U.S.C. §4901 and §4902.
